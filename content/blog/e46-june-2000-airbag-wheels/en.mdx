For most E46 owners, June 2000 is just a date on the build plate. For the steering wheel, it's the line between two generations of airbag wheel. BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, switches the Sedan, Touring and Coupé from one set of airbag-wheel parts to another in that month. [S1, MEDIUM] Here is which cars it touches, which numbers belong on each side, and why it matters when you buy a used wheel. Why BMW's own documents disagree about the date, and everything else about swapping wheels, is in [Your E46 steering wheel is an airbag](/en/blog/e46-steering-wheel-swaps).

## Which cars it touches

In BMW's catalogue, as read on our mirror: [S1, MEDIUM]
- **Sedan, Touring and Coupé:** the older airbag-wheel groups end "to Jun '00", some rows "to Jul '00", and the "Smart" groups start then. On the trees we read, a 328i Sedan shows only the older groups, a 330i only the Smart ones, and a 323Ci Coupé both.
- **Convertible, Compact and M3:** Smart groups only, from the start.

So the question only arises on a Sedan, Touring or Coupé built around mid-2000. BMW's retrofit instruction for the multifunction wheel gives "6/00" on its cover and "as from 06/99" in its text. [S2, HIGH] Until that's settled, the catalogue's June 2000 is the date we work with, and cars built close to it need a look at the parts.

## Two generations, two sets of numbers

The part numbers are what tell the generations apart. In BMW's catalogue, as read on our mirror: [S1, MEDIUM]

| | Older generation, to June/July 2000 | Smart generation, from June/July 2000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard four-spoke leather wheel | 32346753945 | 32346753947 |
| Its hub cap | 32306783783 | 32306757892 or 32306783781 |
| Its connecting lead | not in our notes | 32306750166 |
| Sport leather wheel | 32346753944 | in the Smart sport groups, not restated here |
| Its hub cap | 32301095767 | a Smart hub cap with its own number |

As listed on one mirror, on a 330i Sedan tree for the Smart parts. [S1, MEDIUM] The older groups also hold a "foam plastic airbag" wheel, 32346753946. [S1, MEDIUM]

The practical point: a wheel or hub cap with an older-generation number is the part BMW listed for cars built before the line. If your car was built after it, that wheel isn't the generation BMW listed for your car.

## Which side is your car on

- **The build month decides,** not the registration. A car first registered in late 2000 can have been built before June. The same trap applies to the facelift; see [The 2001 facelift](/en/blog/e46-2001-facelift).
- **Built in June or July 2000:** the catalogue itself shows both months as boundaries, so check the numbers on the parts in your car before you buy.
- **Convertible, Compact or M3:** in the catalogue, Smart from the start. [S1, MEDIUM]

## Why it matters

- **The multifunction retrofit** is only for Smart cars, according to BMW's instruction. [S2, HIGH] How that kit works is in [Buttons without wires](/en/blog/e46-multifunction-steering-wheel-retrofit).
- **At the German HU,** an airbag that is "nicht für das Fahrzeug geeignet", not suitable for the vehicle, is a significant defect in the inspection guideline's defect list, item 7.1.5 a. [S3] Our sources don't say how an inspector judges a wheel from the other side of the line; that item is the reason to match it.
- **A used wheel is a used airbag mount.** Buy it with the part numbers stated, and match them to your car's side of June 2000.

## Notes on the evidence

**Confidence.** The June/July 2000 boundary, the bodies and the part numbers come from one mirror of BMW's parts catalogue, read with its date columns (MEDIUM). The retrofit restriction and the conflicting dates come from BMW's own installation instruction (HIGH for what it says). The inspection item is from the official defect list, read on a mirror (an official text, ungraded).

**Not established.** Why some rows say June and others July. Whether the catalogue's "Smart" and the instruction's "SMART" are exactly the same thing. The Smart sport wheel's own numbers, which we don't restate here. US cars: their catalogue trees weren't read. Whether an older-generation wheel can be made to work on a later car; no BMW source in our research says so.
