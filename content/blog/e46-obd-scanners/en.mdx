A warning light on the dash and a cheap OBD scanner on the seat: it's how most owners start diagnosing an E46. Sometimes the scanner tells you exactly what's wrong. Often it tells you less than you think. Here is what it can reach, and why.

## Two sockets, and one went away

Early E46s, at least in the US, had two diagnostic sockets: a round 20-pin socket under the bonnet, and a 16-pin OBD socket inside the car. [S1, HIGH] According to BMW of North America's training on the 2001 model year, the E46 lost the 20-pin socket from June 2000 production. [S1, HIGH] From then on, the 16-pin OBD socket in the driver's footwell, to the left of the steering column, is the only diagnosis port. [S1, HIGH]

BMW's training also explains why that mattered. Before the change, the 16-pin socket gave access only to emissions-relevant data; BMW's own diagnosis line reached the car through the 20-pin socket. From June 2000, that line runs to the 16-pin socket too. [S1, HIGH]

That training is written for US cars. For European cars, BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, lists both an OBD II socket cover with a 16-pole contact and the engine-bay diagnosis plug parts, but without dates. [S3, MEDIUM] So the exact changeover date for European cars isn't established; check your car: if there's a round socket under the bonnet, it has both.

## What the law asked for

In the EU, on-board diagnostics for emissions (often called EOBD) came with [Directive 98/69/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1998/69/contents/adopted), as we read it. [S2]
- **Petrol cars** must have an OBD system for emission control, with [the directive's dates](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1998/69/article/2/adopted) of 1 January 2000 for new types and 1 January 2001 for all new cars. [S2]
- **Diesel cars** must have one from 1 January 2003 for new types and from 1 January 2004 for all new cars, with later dates for heavier cars and those with more than six seats. [S2]
- **The socket is standardised:** the connection to the tester has to meet an ISO standard, and it has to be readily accessible for service staff. [S2]

So an early diesel E46 may not have been required to have full EOBD. Whether BMW fitted it earlier on a given diesel, the directive can't tell you.

## What a generic scanner reads

The directive describes OBD as a system that must indicate the failure of emission-related components, and for petrol engines it must at least watch things like the catalytic converter, misfires and the oxygen sensors. [S2] That's what a generic OBD or EOBD scanner is built for: the emission-related faults behind the engine warning light, read through the standardised socket. [S2]

On a car with a lit engine warning light, that's often enough to point you to the right area.

## What it can't read

BMW's training separates two things: the emissions-relevant data the standard OBD connection always gave, and BMW's own diagnosis line, which its workshop tools use. [S1, HIGH] A generic scanner is built for the first. The car's other systems, such as the airbag, ABS and DSC, or the body electronics, aren't emission-related, so they're outside what a generic scanner is made to read. That's our reading of how BMW and the directive describe the two kinds of data, not a statement about any particular scanner.

So:
- **An airbag or ABS light** usually needs a tool that speaks BMW's diagnosis, not a generic OBD scanner.
- **Coding** needs BMW's own tools. BMW's instructions for its E46 Bluetooth kit, for example, say the car must be coded afterwards with BMW's workshop diagnosis systems. [S4, HIGH] Our [infotainment article](/en/blog/e46-infotainment-dash) covers which kits need it.

## When a scanner is worth plugging in

- **Before you buy a used E46.** Read the codes, and ask about any that are stored, even if the warning light is off.
- **Before and after fitting parts.** A scan before tells you what was already there; a scan after tells you whether you added anything.
- **When a light comes on.** Read it first. Clearing a code without fixing the cause only switches the light off until it comes back.
