"Is mine xenon?" is the headlight question every E46 owner asks once, usually when a lamp needs replacing or a used pair is on offer. The answer matters, because halogen, xenon and bi-xenon units are not interchangeable without other parts, and on some cars an adaptive unit hides inside a normal-looking housing. The good news is that the possibilities are few once you know your body and build date. This article shows what each technology looks like switched on, which cues owners use, and which ones mislead.

## The short version

- **Body and build date narrow it to two choices.** A Sedan or Touring built before September 2001 has halogen or xenon; from September 2001, halogen or bi-xenon. [S2, HIGH; date MEDIUM]
- **The Coupé and Convertible change twice:** halogen or xenon to September 2001, halogen or bi-xenon after it, and from March 2003 the facelift units, with adaptive headlights as a further option. [S1, HIGH; dates MEDIUM; S6, MEDIUM]
- **The Compact has halogen or bi-xenon, and nothing else.** [S5, HIGH]
- **Xenon and bi-xenon were factory options** [S3, HIGH], so the car's equipment list, with option 522 for xenon, answers it. [S1, MEDIUM]
- **Lit, xenon reads whiter and bluer than halogen.** Owners agree on that; it is the cue most people use, but it is not proof. [S4, LOW]

## What your car can have

The parts catalogue sorts E46 headlights by body and production band. For Europe, two catalogue mirrors agree on the families. [S1, HIGH; S2, HIGH]

| Body | Built | Possible units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sedan, Touring | to September 2001 | halogen, xenon |
| Sedan, Touring | from September 2001 | halogen, bi-xenon |
| Coupé, Convertible | to September 2001 | halogen, xenon |
| Coupé, Convertible | September 2001 to March 2003 | halogen, bi-xenon |
| Coupé, Convertible | from March 2003 | halogen, bi-xenon, adaptive bi-xenon |
| Compact | whole production | halogen, bi-xenon |

The months are the catalogue's production breaks, MEDIUM; the families are HIGH. [S1, S2] BMW's own press kits for model year 2002 confirm the change on the Sedan and Touring: they list bi-xenon among the new options, and the US kit describes it as an upgrade of the optional xenon "to Bi-xenon lights, which include both low and high beams". [S3, HIGH] So "xenon" on an early car and "bi-xenon" on a later one are different units, not two names for one.

Two combinations do not exist in the catalogue at all: a plain (single-beam) xenon unit on a facelift Sedan or Touring, and a plain (single-beam) xenon unit on a Compact. [S2, HIGH; S5, HIGH] If a listing offers one, it is either mislabelled or from another body.

The Coupé and Convertible are trickier. Their visible facelift came in March 2003, when the catalogue's units gain an integrated indicator, but the change from xenon to bi-xenon came earlier, in September 2001, under the old shape. [S1, HIGH; months MEDIUM] So a 2002 Coupé looks early but can have bi-xenon. And a car built in 2001 cannot be placed on either side of the September line from its year alone. [S1, MEDIUM; S2, MEDIUM]

## Cues that work, and cues that mislead

**The build data.** Xenon and bi-xenon were factory options, so the car's equipment list answers the question directly. [S3, HIGH] The catalogue ties xenon to option code 522 and adaptive headlights to 524. [S1, MEDIUM]

**The label on the unit.** If the unit carries its BMW part number on a label, that number identifies it in the catalogue.

**The headlight-height dial.** Owners report that cars with factory xenon have no manual headlight-height dial on the dashboard, because the beam levels itself. [S4, LOW] A useful hint, not proof.

**A round lens inside the headlight: unreliable.** A common forum rule says a projector lens means xenon. Owners report that the facelift Coupé and Convertible have halogen units with a projector lens too, so on those cars a lens proves nothing. [S4, LOW] No BMW source describes how the units look. [S4]

**Adaptive: not visible from outside.** An adaptive unit on a facelift Coupé or Convertible turns its beam into a bend, and its control electronics also run the automatic levelling. [S6, MEDIUM] We found no source that describes how to tell an adaptive unit from a plain bi-xenon unit from the outside. If a used pair is offered as adaptive, ask for the part number.

## Before you buy a headlight

1. **Start with body and build date** and use the table above to narrow it to two choices. [S1, S2]
2. **Check the build data** if you can get it. [S3, HIGH]
3. **Read the label on your own unit** and on the one on offer.
4. **Treat light colour and the dial as hints,** and a lens as no evidence at all on a facelift Coupé or Convertible. [S4, LOW]
5. **Don't mix technologies across a swap** without the parts that go with them; our article on adaptive units explains why. See ["The E46 headlight that works, until it doesn't"](/en/blog/e46-adaptive-headlights-not-a-simple-swap).

The exact changeover dates per body are in ["Two dates decide every E46 headlight"](/en/blog/e46-headlight-changeovers), and the Compact's units in ["The loner"](/en/blog/e46-compact-headlights).

## Notes on the evidence

**Confidence.** Technology per body and band comes from two catalogue mirrors (HIGH for the families, MEDIUM for the months) and from BMW's own press kits. Every visual cue is from owners and a retailer, LOW: no BMW document describes how the units look. The pictures are AI illustrations from zauberlabs' image set; two images in that set were left out, a facelift Sedan with plain xenon and a pre-facelift Sedan with bi-xenon, because the catalogue has no such Sedan, and so were the two facelift Sedan pictures, because they do not show the units correctly; we show no picture where we do not have a correct one.

**Conflicts recorded.** The forum rule "lens means xenon" is contradicted by owners' reports for the facelift Coupé and Convertible halogen units.

**Not established.** What separates TITAN from standard units visually; the exact production month of each change per model; how each unit looks in other markets.
