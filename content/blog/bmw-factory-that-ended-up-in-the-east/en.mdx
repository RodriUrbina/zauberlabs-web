Germany marks the day of its reunification on 3 October. For BMW the date has a specific address: a factory in Eisenach, Thuringia, that built the first BMW cars, spent four decades on the other side of the border building BMWs under another name and then Wartburgs, and saw BMW return in 1992. The E46 that this site is about belongs to the generation that followed. Here is the story, with every date tied to a source and graded.

## The first BMW car factory was in the East

BMW was founded in Munich in 1916 and built aircraft engines and then motorcycles before it built a car. [S4, HIGH] Its first car factory was not in Bavaria. In **November 1928** BMW took over the Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach, a Thuringian works founded at the end of 1896 that had been selling cars under the **Dixi** name. [S1, HIGH for the takeover and the founding; S8, MEDIUM for the Dixi name from 1904] BMW's own plant page still calls Eisenach "the first ever BMW car factory". [S2, HIGH]

<Figure src="/images/map-germany-eisenach.svg" alt="Simplified outline map of Germany with Eisenach marked in Thuringia and Munich shown for contrast" caption="Eisenach, Thuringia." credit="Zauberlabs, own drawing" />

What Eisenach built was small. Dixi had taken a licence for Britain's Austin Seven, and BMW kept only that small car when it bought the works, dropping Dixi's large luxury models. Badged **BMW 3/15**, the little four-cylinder car developed 15 hp at 3,000 rpm, weighed under half a tonne, managed 75 km/h and cost 2,175 Reichsmarks in 1930. The larger BMW 3/20 followed in 1932 as the Austin licence ran out. [S3, HIGH]

## 1945: the factory stays, BMW leaves

The end of the war put Eisenach in the Soviet occupation zone. American troops reached the plant in **April 1945**; Soviet forces took it over in **July 1945**, and Thuringia became part of what would be East Germany. [S9, MEDIUM; S11, MEDIUM for the exact days, 6 April and 3 July] More than half the plant was destroyed. [S11, MEDIUM]

It did not stay silent for long. A Soviet order of **13 October 1945**, which the regional press credits with saving the Eisenach car-making site, put the former BMW works back to building cars and motorcycles, and BMW's own account confirms that **production began again in November 1945**. [S11, MEDIUM for the order and its date; S1, HIGH for the restart] By the end of the year the first 68 cars and 75 motorcycles had left the halls: pre-war BMW designs, built in the East, still wearing the BMW badge. [S11, MEDIUM] After the war the plant was run as a Soviet-owned stock company; sales to private customers and export began in 1948. [S8, S9, MEDIUM]

<Figure src="/blog/bmw-factory-that-ended-up-in-the-east/bmw-321.webp" alt="A pale green BMW 321 two-door saloon from the late 1930s" width={1207} height={759} caption="BMW 321: one of the pre-war BMW designs the plant went on building after 1945, at first still badged BMW." credit="Photo: Schwer13, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons" />

BMW in Munich had meanwhile lost its only car factory. BMW's first post-war car, the 501, was "produced from 1952". [S4, HIGH] For seven years, in other words, the only new "BMW" cars in the world were coming out of a plant BMW no longer owned.

<Figure src="/blog/bmw-factory-that-ended-up-in-the-east/bmw-501.webp" alt="A white BMW 501 saloon in a museum hall" width={1600} height={1067} caption="BMW 501: BMW's first post-war car, from 1952." credit="Photo: 先従隗始, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons" />

## EMW, then Wartburg

That could not last. After a lawsuit in **1952**, the Eisenach works stopped using the BMW name and became **EMW**, Eisenacher Motorenwerk, its roundel recoloured from Bavarian blue and white to red and white. The same year the plant passed into East German state ownership. [S8, S9, MEDIUM] EMW kept building the pre-war-derived six-cylinder cars for a few more years, among them the EMW 340, produced from 1952 to 1955. [S9, MEDIUM]

<Figure src="/blog/bmw-factory-that-ended-up-in-the-east/emw-340.webp" alt="A dark red EMW 340 saloon at a classic-car event" width={1600} height={1200} caption="EMW 340, built 1952 to 1955: a pre-war BMW design under the Eisenach works' new name and red-and-white badge." credit="Photo: Kirchenfan, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons" />

In **1956** the old Wartburg name was revived for a new front-wheel-drive car, the 311, and a year later the plant took the name VEB Automobilwerk Eisenach. The Wartburg 353 followed in the mid-1960s and the Wartburg 1.3 in 1988. [S8, S10, MEDIUM] The plant that had built the first BMWs now built East Germany's mid-size car, and did so for thirty-five years.

## 1990 to 1992: the Wall falls, the Wartburg stops, BMW returns

Reunification caught the Eisenach works at the end of its road. The **last Wartburg was built on 10 April 1991**, and the Treuhand agency, which wound up East German state enterprises, closed Automobilwerk Eisenach in **April 1991**. [S10, S8, MEDIUM] Opel opened a new plant on the edge of the town in 1992. [S8, MEDIUM]

BMW came back too, though not to build cars. On **10 March 1992** BMW opened **BMW Fahrzeugtechnik GmbH** in Eisenach, in BMW's own words returning "to its roots" in the town; the company invested DM 120 million and describes itself as the first major investor in eastern Germany after the fall of the Wall. [S1, HIGH for the date, the investment and BMW's own description] The plant made, and still makes, the large press tools that give BMW, MINI and Rolls-Royce bodies their shape, and sheet-metal body parts. [S1, S2, HIGH] BMW's plant page puts it plainly: "After German reunification, the BMW Group returned to Eisenach in 1992". [S2, HIGH]

## Where the 3 Series stood in 1990

While the Eisenach works were building their last Wartburgs, BMW in Munich was launching the car that would carry it through the decade. The **third-generation 3 Series, the E36, arrived in 1990**, a four-door Sedan first. [S5, S7, HIGH] BMW's own histories date the Coupé to 1992, the Convertible to 1993 (BMW's US text of 2011 says only that it followed the Coupé by a few months), the Compact to 1994 and the Touring to 1995. [S5, S6, S7, HIGH]

<Figure src="/blog/bmw-factory-that-ended-up-in-the-east/bmw-e36.webp" alt="A silver BMW E36 3 Series four-door saloon of the early 1990s" width={1600} height={1101} caption="BMW 3 Series, third generation (E36), the car BMW launched in 1990." credit="Photo: Bull-Doser, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons" />

The E36 was the 3 Series of the reunification decade, and its successor is the car this site exists for. The **fourth generation, the E46, arrived in 1998**, in BMW's words "in the spring of 1998", with the Sedan; BMW's US press material gives May 1998. The Coupé followed in April 1999 and the Touring later that year, the Convertible in spring 2000 and the Compact in early 2001. [S5, S6, HIGH; S7 for May 1998]

<Figure src="/blog/bmw-factory-that-ended-up-in-the-east/bmw-e46.webp" alt="A dark blue BMW E46 3 Series saloon, facelift version, parked" width={1572} height={936} caption="BMW 3 Series, fourth generation (E46): the car this site is about." credit="Photo: Rudolf Stricker, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons" />

BMW's own figures: **2,745,780 E36** (BMW Group Classic says "sold"; BMW Group's 2025 article rounds it to 2.75 million "rolled off the production line" by 2000) and **3,266,885 E46** by 2006, given in both documents as the 3 Series' historical peak. [S5, S6, HIGH]

## Why this matters for an E46 owner

Nothing in this story changes a part number. It does explain two things owners meet. First, why BMW's own history calls Eisenach its first car plant and why a press tool for a 3 Series body panel may have been made in Thuringia: the plant BMW returned to in 1992 is the one it lost in 1945. [S1, S2, HIGH] Second, why the E46 is a product of a particular moment: designed in the years after reunification, launched in 1998 by a company that had just rebuilt its presence in the East. The configurator deals in the car's parts; this is the country they were built in.
