Every car configurator answers the same question: what will my car look like, and what will it cost? Over the years the answer has moved from paper to the screen to a conversation. This is a short history of that move, told through the makers' own documents, and of the one question it still leaves out.

## Paper first: the price list and the colour card

Before screens, you configured a BMW with a pencil. The German price lists we hold for the E46 run from September 1998 to March 2006. [S1, HIGH] They are dense tables: every option a line, every line a price, standard or extra marked per model. Paint and upholstery weren't in the table at all: the lists send you to a separate colour and trim card. [S1, HIGH] A 1998 BMW brochure from our collection ends the same way, with its own colour card on the last pages. [S2, HIGH]

So the first configurator was you: a price list on your knee, a colour card in your hand, and the dealer to tell you what went with what.

## The configurator goes online

By November 1999, bmw.de had a page called "BMW CarConfigurator". [S8, HIGH] A capture from January 2000 shows what it offered: put together the BMW "der genau zu Ihnen paßt", save it to your personal parking space, compare equipment variants, see a finance offer from BMW Financial Services, and print it to talk it over with friends or your BMW dealer. [S9, HIGH] It was optimised for Netscape 4.0, and its footer reads "© 1999 BMW Deutschland". [S9, HIGH] That's the E46's own first years, on the web.

Mercedes-Benz dates the start of its car configurator to 1997. [S3, HIGH] Neither date is a "first": an archived page proves only that it existed by then.

## Pictures per choice: rendered in advance

Choose a colour, and the car on the screen changes. Often, what you see is not a car being drawn for you but a picture made in advance for that exact combination.

Some configurators still work this way. When we looked at Volkswagen's configurator for the ID. Polo on 14 November 2026, it loaded around 394 ready-made images from Volkswagen's media server, each address naming the model, trim, colour and wheels, in fixed camera views such as side and three-quarter. We saw no 3D model and no 3D engine loading. [S4, MEDIUM] Its "360°" view, as we saw it, is a flip-book of those pictures.

## Into the headset, then real time

In January 2015 Audi brought the configurator to the dealer in a VR headset: customers could configure their car there, with the whole model range "including all possible equipment combinations". [S5, HIGH] Audi called itself the first carmaker to build retail software for VR headsets; that's its own claim. [S5, HIGH]

In September 2020 BMW described the next step in plain words. Every BMW and MINI model can be reconstructed with any optional equipment, "rendered in real-time" and shown to the customer: on a screen at the dealer, in VR goggles in the showroom, or through a link at home. [S6, HIGH] The car had stopped being a set of pictures made in advance and become a 3D object you could turn.

## Today: describe it, don't click it

In July 2026 BMW put its configurator into ChatGPT. You describe what you want, by text or by voice: space, ground clearance, drivetrain, how you'll use it. The plugin suggests models and configurations, and the result opens in the BMW Configurator. [S7, HIGH] BMW calls itself the first carmaker to offer vehicle advice directly in ChatGPT; that, too, is its own claim. [S7, HIGH]

Mercedes-Benz, for its part, says its configurator team is "exploring" artificial intelligence. [S3, HIGH]

## The gap: they all stop at the sale

Look back over the story, from price list to chatbot. The configurators in it all build a car that doesn't exist yet. They answer "which new car should I order?", and once the car is delivered they have nothing more to say.

But most cars on the road were configured years ago. Their owners have a different question: *what can my car become, and which parts actually fit it?*

That's where zauberlabs begins: "See what your car could be — and buy only what fits it." E46BUILD starts from the car you already have. You pick its body style, then answer only what a part's fit depends on, such as the build year or whether it's a facelift. [S10]

The price list, the colour card and the configurator were all about the day you ordered. For an owner, that day is long past. The interesting question is the next one.
