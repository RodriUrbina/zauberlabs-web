Two used bi-xenon headlights from a facelift E46 Coupé look the same from the outside. One of them may be an **adaptive** unit, the kind that turns its low beam into the bend as you steer. The other is the plain bi-xenon. Both bolt into the same hole and take the same bulb. The evidence we have says they are **not interchangeable in either direction**, and the reason is not the swivel. It is the beam levelling.

Everything below is graded. **HIGH** means two BMW catalogue mirrors print it, or it is settled regulation. **MEDIUM** means a BMW-authored text that we could only read second-hand, or a single catalogue mirror. **LOW** means owners' forums or retailers, shown as their claim, not as fact. Sources are listed at the end.

## The short version

- Adaptive headlights on the E46 exist only on the **facelift Coupé and Convertible**, only together with **bi-xenon**, and appear on the catalogue's facelift diagrams, which start in **March 2003**. [S1, HIGH for the xenon dependency; S2, MEDIUM for "Coupé and Convertible only"]
- According to BMW's own training material, the adaptive control unit **"is also responsible for the automatic headlight-range adjustment"**, and the motor controllers inside each adaptive headlight drive **both** the swivel and the levelling. [S2, MEDIUM]
- An **adaptive unit in a car without the system**: it lights, it does not swivel, and the **automatic levelling does not work**. [S2 mechanism, MEDIUM; S4 reports, LOW]
- A **plain unit in an adaptive car**: it lights, the car **stores a fault**, and the **levelling does not work**. [S4, LOW, several concordant threads]
- Automatic levelling is a **legal requirement** for xenon low beams in ECE markets, and German inspection guides class a non-working levelling as a **significant defect**. [S3, MEDIUM for the requirement; LOW–MEDIUM for the inspection outcome]
- **How to check your car:** do the low beams turn when you steer at night? [owner-testable; S4 for the switch-on sweep, LOW]

## What "adaptive" means on an E46

BMW's option is called **Adaptive Headlights** (option code 524 in the catalogue headers; BMW's internal names are AKL, AHL and ALC). On the catalogue diagrams of the facelift Coupé and Convertible, every "bi-xenon headlight AKL" row sits under two conditions: **Xenon Light** *and* **Adaptive Headlights**. There is no adaptive halogen unit and no adaptive plain-xenon unit. [S1, HIGH: both mirrors, Europe and USA]

In Europe the adaptive units are listed only in the **TITAN** trim (the lighter inner finish), with and without white indicator lenses. [S1, MEDIUM: Europe rows on both mirrors, graded MEDIUM in the headlight table]

What the system does, per BMW's training text: below about 50 km/h the headlights follow the **steering angle**; above that they follow the **yaw rate** from the stability-control sensor and the road speed; the light switch must be in the automatic position. [S2, MEDIUM] The parts that make this happen are a separate **adaptive control unit** in the car (on the E46 Coupé and Convertible only; its catalogue number was not established), a **stepper-motor controller inside each headlight**, and the ride-height sensors that feed it. [S2, MEDIUM; S4 for the control unit's location behind the glove box, LOW]

The catalogue has a dedicated diagram for the adaptive-specific parts, "single components for headlight xenon / ALC". Its index entry is on the saved pages; the diagram itself was not saved. [S1, HIGH that it exists; contents not established]

## Why the levelling is the problem

Every xenon car has automatic beam levelling: sensors on the axles read the car's pitch and motors in the headlights keep the beam at the right height. On a **plain** bi-xenon E46 the light control module drives a conventional levelling motor in each headlight directly.

On an **adaptive** E46 the architecture is different. BMW's training text says the adaptive control unit, "for safety reasons", **also owns the automatic headlight-range adjustment**, and that the stepper-motor controllers in the headlights "control the stepper motors in the headlights for the automatic headlight-range adjustment and for the adaptive headlights". [S2, MEDIUM] In plain words: on an adaptive car, up-and-down and side-to-side are run by the same electronics, and the car has no direct levelling drive of its own.

Owners who have opened the wiring describe the consequence: the headlight connector position that carries the plain levelling motor on a standard car carries a two-wire data bus to the stepper controller on an adaptive car. [S4, LOW] Same plug, different signal.

## Adaptive unit into a plain car

What the evidence says happens: [S2 for the mechanism, MEDIUM; S4 for the reports, LOW]

- **It lights.** Ballast, igniter and bulb are not part of the headlight unit in the catalogue, and the diagram's note that the xenon headlight "does not comprise ignition control unit and xenon light bulb" applies to adaptive and plain rows alike. [S1, HIGH]
- **It does not swivel.** There is no adaptive control unit to tell it to.
- **The levelling does not work.** The adaptive unit's levelling is a stepper motor behind its own controller; the plain car's light module cannot command it.
- One owner warns that connecting the plain car's levelling plug to an adaptive unit **destroyed the stepper electronics**. [S4, LOW, one thread]
- **No warning lamp is expected**, because there is no module to report the fault. [S4, LOW]

A full retrofit, according to owners who have done it, needs the adaptive control unit, a different light control module, the data-bus wiring, re-routed level sensors and coding of the option into the car. [S4, LOW] The scope of BMW's own **xenon** retrofit kit (sensors, loom, brackets, labels, no headlights) shows the baseline a xenon conversion already needs; an adaptive conversion needs that and more. [S5, LOW]

**No BMW retrofit kit for adaptive headlights was found.** Earlier research read a catalogue entry called "retrofit kit cornering light" as an adaptive-headlight kit. The later review found that it is **probably the static cornering light** (German *Abbiegelicht*, an extra lamp that lights when you indicate or steer at low speed), a different function with its own BMW installation instruction for the Compact and Coupé from March 2003. A retailer states adaptive cornering light "can only be retrofitted by replacing the main headlights and control units". [S5, S6: LOW–MEDIUM, pending a read of the kit's catalogue diagram] We do not present that kit as an adaptive retrofit.

## Plain unit into an adaptive car

The reverse case rests on owners' reports only, but several independent threads agree: [S4, LOW]

- **It lights.**
- The adaptive control unit **stores a stepper-motor fault**; a warning or a blinking fault LED on the light module is reported.
- **The levelling does not work**, because the adaptive car has no direct levelling drive.
- The reported workaround is to **code the adaptive option out** of the car, re-route the level sensors to the light module and fit the non-adaptive light module. That is a module swap plus coding, not a headlight swap.

We found no BMW document on this direction at all. [S6]

## Why this is more than a convenience problem

- **Automatic levelling is mandatory** for gas-discharge (xenon) low beams in ECE markets: Regulation 48 requires the levelling device to be automatic for light sources above 2000 lumen, and headlamp cleaning likewise. [S3, MEDIUM: paragraph numbers from summaries, not read verbatim]
- German periodic inspection (HU) guides classify **non-functioning automatic levelling on a xenon car as a significant defect** ("erheblicher Mangel"), which means a re-inspection, and a clear fail if the beam is also mis-aimed. [S3, LOW–MEDIUM: inspection guides, not the official defect catalogue]
- The headlamp itself keeps its type approval: a swivelling headlamp at rest is still an approved headlamp. **Whether an inoperative swivel function is itself recorded at inspection is not established.** [S3, S6]

So the risk of a wrong pair is not "it looks odd". It is a car that no longer meets a lighting requirement, with an inspection consequence that depends on your country and your inspector. Treat the inspection outcome as likely, not certain: the evidence is MEDIUM at best.

## How to tell whether your car has adaptive headlights

1. **Watch the low beams at night while you steer.** Adaptive units turn visibly into the bend; plain units do not. This is the test the system is built around. [S2 for the function, MEDIUM]
2. **Switch the lights on with the ignition on.** Owners report that adaptive units perform a short sweep when switched on. [S4, LOW, harmless if wrong]
3. **Check your car's build data.** The factory option is "Adaptive Headlights", option 524; a BMW dealer can read the options a car left the factory with from its vehicle data. If it is there, your car was built adaptive. If it is not, someone may still have retrofitted it; the night test decides. [S1, HIGH for the option name and code; the dealer lookup is general knowledge, not from the evidence pack]
4. One owner reports that the projector lens on adaptive units reads "DYNAMIC" rather than "XENON". **Uncorroborated**; do not rely on it. [S4, LOW, one thread]

If your car is a Sedan, Touring or Compact, or any pre-facelift E46, this article does not apply to you: adaptive headlights were never offered on those cars. [S2, MEDIUM; S1 for the catalogue rows appearing only on facelift Coupé/Convertible diagrams, HIGH]

## What does *not* matter

- **White or amber indicator lenses.** Regulation 48 requires amber *light* from indicators, and the white-lens units reach it with an amber bulb; the Europe catalogue lists them for the Europe region, so they are approved factory parts. No technical dependency on the adaptive system. Cosmetic, pick what you like. [S3, HIGH for the amber rule; S1, HIGH for the Europe listing]
- **TITAN or black inner trim.** Cosmetic. [S1]
- **Ballast, igniter, bulb.** Separate parts, the same for adaptive and plain units. [S1, HIGH]

## Before you buy a pair

1. **Establish what your car is**: adaptive or plain, by the night test and the build data.
2. **Buy like for like.** Adaptive to adaptive, plain to plain. The configurator tells you which is which; on the E46 it only ever matters for the facelift Coupé and Convertible with bi-xenon.
3. **If a seller says "fits all facelift coupés"**, that is true for the bolt holes and false for the electronics. Ask whether the unit is adaptive.
4. **Do not plug a plain levelling connector into an adaptive unit** to "test it". One owner reports the stepper electronics did not survive. [S4, LOW]
5. **Do not plan an adaptive retrofit around a headlight.** It is a control unit, a light module, wiring, sensors and coding, and BMW published no kit for it that we could find.
