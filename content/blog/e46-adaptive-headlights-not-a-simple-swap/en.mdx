Two used bi-xenon headlights from a facelift E46 Coupé look the same from the outside. One of them may be an **adaptive** unit, the kind that turns its low beam into the bend as you steer. The other is the plain bi-xenon. By owners' accounts both bolt in the same way and take the same bulb. [S4, LOW] The evidence we have says they are **not interchangeable in either direction**, and the reason is not the swivel. It is the beam levelling.

## The short version

- Adaptive headlights on the E46 exist only on the **facelift Coupé and Convertible**, only together with **bi-xenon**, on the catalogue diagrams that begin in March 2003. [S1, HIGH for the xenon dependency; MEDIUM for the date; S2, MEDIUM for "Coupé and Convertible only"]
- According to BMW's own system description of the feature, read through a third-party reproduction, the adaptive control unit **"is also responsible for the automatic headlight-range adjustment"**, and the motor controllers inside each adaptive headlight drive **both** the swivel and the levelling. [S2, MEDIUM]
- An **adaptive unit in a car without the system**: it lights, it does not swivel, and the **automatic levelling does not work**. [S2 mechanism, MEDIUM; S4 reports, LOW]
- A **plain unit in an adaptive car**: it lights, the car **stores a fault**, and the **levelling does not work**. [S4, LOW, several concordant threads]
- Automatic levelling is a **legal requirement** for xenon low beams in ECE markets, and German inspection guides class a non-working levelling as a **significant defect**. [S3, MEDIUM for the requirement; LOW–MEDIUM for the inspection outcome]
- **How to check your car:** do the low beams turn when you steer at night?

## What "adaptive" means on an E46

BMW's option is called **Adaptive Headlights** (option code 524 in the catalogue headers; BMW's internal names are AKL, AHL and ALC). On the catalogue diagrams of the facelift Coupé and Convertible, every "bi-xenon headlight AKL" row sits under two conditions: **Xenon Light** *and* **Adaptive Headlights**. There is no adaptive halogen unit and no adaptive plain-xenon unit. [S1, HIGH: both mirrors, Europe and USA]

In Europe the adaptive units are listed only in the **TITAN** inner-trim finish (versus the standard black), with and without white indicator lenses. [S1, MEDIUM: Europe rows, graded MEDIUM in the headlight table]

What the system does, per BMW's description: at town speeds the headlights follow the steering angle, above that the yaw rate from the stability-control sensor, with the light switch in the automatic position. [S2, MEDIUM] The parts that make this happen are a separate **adaptive control unit** in the car (its catalogue entry was not established), a **stepper-motor controller inside each headlight**, and the ride-height sensors that feed it. [S2, MEDIUM; S4 for the control unit's location behind the glove box, LOW] The catalogue has a dedicated diagram for the adaptive-specific parts, "single components for headlight xenon / ALC"; its index entry is on the saved pages, the diagram itself is not. [S1, HIGH that it exists; contents not established]

## Why the levelling is the problem

Every xenon car has automatic beam levelling, because the lighting regulation requires it for gas-discharge low beams: sensors on the axles read the car's pitch and motors in the headlights keep the beam at the right height. [S3, MEDIUM] On a **plain** bi-xenon E46, owners who have traced the wiring say the light control module drives a conventional levelling motor in each headlight directly. [S4, LOW, with the research's inference]

On an **adaptive** E46 the architecture is different. BMW's system description says the adaptive control unit, "for safety reasons", **is also responsible for the automatic headlight-range adjustment**, and that the stepper-motor controllers "control the stepper motors in the headlights for the automatic headlight-range adjustment and for the adaptive headlights". [S2, MEDIUM] Which means: on an adaptive car, up-and-down and side-to-side are run by the same electronics, and the car has no direct levelling drive of its own. [S6, inference from S2, MEDIUM]

Owners who have opened the wiring describe the consequence: the headlight connector position that carries the plain levelling motor on a standard car carries a two-wire data bus to the stepper controller on an adaptive car. [S4, LOW] Same plug, different signal.

## The two wrong swaps, side by side

| | Adaptive unit into a plain car | Plain unit into an adaptive car |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low and high beam | Works. Ballast, igniter and bulb are separate parts, the same for adaptive and plain rows. [S1, HIGH] | Works. [S4, LOW] |
| Swivel | Idle; nothing commands it. [S4, LOW] | None; a plain unit has no swivel. |
| Automatic levelling | **Does not work**: the adaptive unit's levelling is a stepper motor behind its own controller, which the plain car's light module cannot command. [S2 mechanism, MEDIUM; S4 reports, LOW] | **Does not work**: the adaptive car has no direct levelling drive. [S4, LOW; S2 mechanism, MEDIUM] |
| Fault stored / warning | None expected; there is no module to report it. [S4, LOW] | The adaptive control unit stores a stepper-motor fault; a warning or blinking fault LED on the light module is reported. [S4, LOW, several threads] |
| Risk of damage | One owner reports that plugging the plain levelling connector into an adaptive unit destroyed its stepper electronics. [S4, LOW, one thread] | Not reported. |
| What a full conversion needs | Adaptive control unit, a different light control module, data-bus wiring, re-routed level sensors, coding of the option into the car. [S4, LOW] | Code the adaptive option out, re-route the level sensors to the light module, fit the non-adaptive light module: a module swap plus coding. [S4, LOW] |
| BMW documentation | No BMW retrofit kit for adaptive headlights was found. [S6] | No BMW document on this direction at all. [S6] |

A word on BMW's own kits. The scope of BMW's **xenon** retrofit kit (sensors, loom, brackets, labels, no headlights) shows the baseline a xenon conversion already needs; an adaptive conversion needs that and more. [S5, LOW] Earlier research read a catalogue entry called "retrofit kit cornering light" as an adaptive-headlight kit. The later review found that it is **probably the static cornering light** (German *Abbiegelicht*, an extra lamp that lights statically when turning), a different function with its own BMW installation instruction for the Compact and Coupé from March 2003. A retailer states adaptive cornering light "can only be retrofitted by replacing the main headlights and control units". [S5, S6: LOW–MEDIUM, pending a read of the kit's catalogue diagram] We do not present that kit as an adaptive retrofit.

## Why this is more than a convenience problem

- **Automatic levelling is mandatory** for gas-discharge (xenon) low beams in ECE markets: Regulation 48 requires the levelling device to be automatic for light sources above 2000 lumen, and headlamp cleaning likewise. [S3, MEDIUM: paragraph numbers from summaries, not read verbatim]
- German periodic inspection (HU) guides classify **non-functioning automatic levelling on a xenon car as a significant defect** ("erheblicher Mangel"), which means a re-inspection, and a clear fail if the beam is also mis-aimed. The headlamp itself keeps its type approval; whether an inoperative swivel is itself recorded at inspection is not established. [S3, LOW–MEDIUM: inspection guides, not the official defect catalogue]

So the risk of a wrong pair is not "it looks odd". It is a car that no longer meets a lighting requirement, with an inspection consequence that depends on your country and your inspector. Treat the inspection outcome as likely, not certain: the evidence is MEDIUM at best.

## How to tell whether your car has adaptive headlights

1. **Watch the low beams at night while you steer.** Adaptive units turn visibly into the bend; plain units do not. This is the test the system is built around. [S2 for the function, MEDIUM]
2. **Switch the lights on with the ignition on.** Owners report that adaptive units perform a short sweep when switched on. [S4, LOW, harmless if wrong]

If your car is a Sedan, Touring or Compact, or any pre-facelift E46, this article does not apply to you: BMW's text names the Coupé and Convertible only, and no adaptive row appears on any Sedan, Touring or Compact diagram we read. [S2, MEDIUM; S1 for the absence, MEDIUM]

## What does *not* matter

White or amber indicator lenses, TITAN or black inner trim, and the ballast, igniter and bulb are all independent of the adaptive system. The white-lens units are listed for the Europe region, so they are type-approved and must emit amber light; how the bulb achieves that is not recorded in the catalogue. [S1, HIGH for the listing; S3, HIGH for the amber rule; bulb type NOT ESTABLISHED]

## Before you buy a pair

1. **Establish what your car is**: adaptive or plain, by the night test and, if in doubt, the car's factory option list (option 524, "Adaptive Headlights"). [S1, HIGH for the option name and code]
2. **Buy like for like.** Adaptive to adaptive, plain to plain. On the E46 this only ever matters for the facelift Coupé and Convertible with bi-xenon.
3. **If a seller says "fits all facelift coupés"**, that is true for the bolt holes and false for the electronics. Ask whether the unit is adaptive.
4. **Do not plug a plain levelling connector into an adaptive unit** to "test it". One owner reports the stepper electronics did not survive. [S4, LOW]
5. **Do not plan an adaptive retrofit around a headlight.** It is a control unit, a light module, wiring, sensors and coding, and BMW published no kit for it that we could find. [S6]

The configurator does not yet separate adaptive from plain units; ask the seller.
