Search for "E46 sport seats" and you will find hundreds of listings. Very few of them say the three things you need to know about a front seat for a Sedan or a Touring: **which class it is, how it adjusts, and which body it came from**. This article sets out the four seats BMW's parts catalogue knows for these two bodies, adds the ALPINA seats that turn up in the same searches, and shows how to read a listing that leaves the class out, which most do.

## The short version

- **Four combinations.** The catalogue's front seats for the Sedan and Touring come in two classes, **basic seat** and **BMW sports seat**, each **mechanically** or **electrically** adjustable. [S1, MEDIUM]
- **Sedan and Touring share their seat rails.** A front seat from one fits the other's rails; Coupé and Compact seats use a different rail. [S1, MEDIUM; S5, HIGH]
- **Electric sport seats are hard to find.** A search of European sellers in October 2026 found **no** Sedan or Touring front seat whose listing said sport, electric and Sedan or Touring at once, except one ALPINA interior. [S2, LOW]
- **Most listings do not state the class.** A part number in the listing, where there is one, points to the rail and therefore to manual or electric, not to basic or sport. [S5, MEDIUM; S2, LOW]
- **ALPINA is a separate manufacturer.** Whether an ALPINA seat is a BMW sports seat with ALPINA trim is not established by anything we found. [S3, HIGH for ALPINA's status]

## The four seats in the catalogue

For the E46 Sedan and Touring the parts catalogue has four front-seat groups: a mechanically and an electrically adjustable **basic seat**, and a mechanically and an electrically adjustable **BMW sports seat**. Each class has its own frame, rail-mechanism and controls groups. [S1, MEDIUM] As we explained in ["There is no such thing as a 'sport seat'"](/en/blog/e46-sport-seats-what-you-actually-buy), for these two bodies the catalogue does not sell a complete seat; what it lists are frames, rails, controls, head restraints and covers. A complete seat is therefore something you buy used. [S1, MEDIUM]

Two other words in listings are not classes. "**Comfort seat**" on an E46 is a basic or sports seat with options such as electric adjustment, heating or lumbar support, usually with leather. "**Recaro**" is a manufacturer; its seats are aftermarket and not covered here. [S1, MEDIUM]

## What tells a sports seat from a basic seat

The catalogue lists separate head restraints for the two classes. Underneath, the sports seat differs by its own seat pan, springs and a carrier for the thigh support, coded as a separate option; the rails are common to both classes. [S1, MEDIUM; S5, MEDIUM] In practice owners look at three things:

- **Side bolsters.** The sports seat holds you at the sides of the backrest and the cushion; the basic seat is flatter.
- **Thigh support.** The sports seat has a cushion extension at the front edge, the part the catalogue's extra carrier holds. [S5, MEDIUM] Seller texts for sport pairs name it ("Oberschenkelauflage", thigh support) and say whether it adjusts by hand or electrically. [S2, LOW for what the listings say]
- **Head restraint shape**, which differs by class. [S1, MEDIUM]

None of this is in the listing title, usually. It is in the photos and, sometimes, in the description. If a seller calls a seat "sport" and the photos show a flat cushion without an extension, ask.

## Manual or electric: the rail tells you

Here the catalogue helps more. The **seat rail mechanism** for the Sedan and Touring comes in a mechanical and an electric version, with different numbers, and both mirrors agree that the Sedan and Touring use the same ones. The electric rails changed number in March 2003. [S1, MEDIUM; S5, HIGH] Some dismantlers print a BMW number in their listings, and in our October search those numbers were rail numbers: one pointed to the Sedan/Touring **manual** rail, another to an **electric** rail. [S2, LOW for the listings; S1, MEDIUM for what the numbers are]

That is useful, and it has limits:

- The rail number tells you **manual or electric**. It does **not** tell you **basic or sport**, because both classes use the same rails. [S5, MEDIUM]
- It tells you the **body group** the rail belongs to, which matters because a Coupé or Compact rail is not a Sedan or Touring rail. [S1, MEDIUM]
- A seller's number is the seller's claim. The seat in the photo may not match it.

Electric seats also have their own wiring set in the catalogue. A manual seat in an electric car, or an electric seat in a manual car, means wiring work. [S1, MEDIUM] Heating is the one option the catalogue makes simple: the heated-seat installation set carries the same numbers on Sedan, Touring, Coupé and Compact. [S1, MEDIUM]

## What a search actually found

In early October 2026 our research checked eight European sellers of used and genuine BMW parts for one thing: a Sedan or Touring **electric sport** front seat. The rule was strict: the seller's own text had to state the body, the sport class and electric adjustment. Photos and numbers alone did not count. [S2, LOW]

The result, as published by the sellers on those days: [S2, LOW]

- **No BMW Sedan or Touring electric sport seat** at any seller whose pages could be read.
- One **manual sport pair** for the Sedan and Touring, leather, heated, with hand-adjusted thigh support, on sale for a customer.
- Several **electric** sets whose text said electric and memory but **did not state the class**. One Touring set appeared at two sellers with identical text, from the same donor car: one seller appears to resell the other's stock.
- One set with a number pointing to the **manual** rail and no remarks at all.
- The single listing that met all three conditions was an **ALPINA** interior.

Two sellers' pages load their results only after a JavaScript check; we did not get past that and did not count them. One of them showed a title promising Sedan and Touring sport seats, electric, at a price that looks low for that description; we could not read the page, so it proves nothing. [S2, LOW]

This is a snapshot, not a census. It does suggest that a buyer who wants **factory electric sport seats** for a Sedan or Touring should expect to look for a while, and should read every listing for the three facts above.

## The ALPINA question

The one listing that ticked every box came from an **ALPINA B3**: a full interior with front seats described as "original ALPINA leather sports seats with electric adjustment and seat heating", driver memory, adjustable thigh support, and an ALPINA rear bench, in black leather, from a 1999 car. [S2, LOW for the listing text]

What is an ALPINA? According to BMW's own press release of 2022, ALPINA is a vehicle manufacturer based in Buchloe in Bavaria, which began building passenger cars **based on BMW products in 1978** and has been **registered as an automotive manufacturer since 1983**. BMW describes base BMW cars receiving extensive modification by ALPINA, with final assembly in Buchloe. [S3, HIGH] ALPINA's model history, as summarised by a search engine, lists the B3 3.3 as based on the 328i and 330i and built from 1999 to 2002 as Saloon, Touring, Coupé and Convertible. [S4, LOW: the page now redirects and could not be read]

So an ALPINA B3 seat is an ALPINA part from an ALPINA car built on an E46. What it is **underneath** we cannot say:

- No source we found states whether the B3's front seats are **BMW sports-seat structures with ALPINA trim** or something of ALPINA's own.
- The listing gives **no part number**, so the rail cannot be checked.
- It is a **set**, front pair and rear bench, which is a different purchase from a front pair.

If the rails and wiring turn out to be BMW Sedan or Touring electric parts, it may bolt in like any other Sedan or Touring seat. That is a question to put to the seller, with photos of the rails and the plugs under the seat, before you buy. We do not answer it here.

## Before you buy a used pair

1. **Body.** Sedan or Touring? The rails interchange; Coupé and Compact rails do not. [S5, HIGH]
2. **Class.** Look for side bolsters, the thigh-support extension and the head restraint. If the listing says nothing, ask for side-on photos. [S1, MEDIUM]
3. **Adjustment.** Manual or electric, and does it match your car? A rail number in the listing answers this, if it is one. [S1, MEDIUM; S2, LOW]
4. **Options.** Heating, memory and lumbar support each add wiring; heating is the simplest retrofit. [S1, MEDIUM]
5. **What comes with it.** Rails, head restraints, belt buckles, the wiring under the seat. A set may include the rear bench.
6. **The airbag side.** The seat connects to the car's airbag system through the belt tensioner and, on the passenger side, the occupancy sensor. Our article ["The airbag is in the door"](/en/blog/e46-seat-swap-airbags) covers what to check after fitting.
7. **ALPINA.** Treat it as its own product until the rails and wiring are confirmed. [S3, HIGH for ALPINA's status]

## Notes on the evidence

**Confidence.** The seat classes, groups and retrofit sets come from one parts-catalogue mirror with no model or year applicability; the shared Sedan and Touring rails are confirmed by a second mirror at part-number level. The market search is a set of seller listings read on two days in October 2026, graded LOW: listings say what the seller wrote, and they change. What ALPINA is comes from BMW's own press release. The B3's base models and dates come only from a search summary of ALPINA's model-history page, which no longer loads.

**Conflicts recorded.** (1) An earlier pass of the same market research recorded the ALPINA listing's class as "not stated" and no price; both were extraction misses, corrected. (2) One seller's sitemap no longer listed a set that was still on sale on its own page; absence from a sitemap is not evidence of a sale.

**Not established.** Which Sedan and Touring models had sports or electric seats as standard or option in which year; whether ALPINA B3 front seats share BMW's sports-seat frame and rails; whether two pages we could not read hold Sedan or Touring electric sport seats.
