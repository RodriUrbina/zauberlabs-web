Look at the boot lid of some E46 Coupés and you'll see it: a thin lip along the trailing edge. BMW's Clubsport package carried one. [S1, HIGH] It's a small part with a surprising amount of confusion around it: which Coupés it fits, whether it should come painted, and what it's even called.

## One part in BMW's catalogue

BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, has one rear spoiler in the E46 Coupé's group: an "M spoiler rear", sold **primed**, number 51717893671 ("M Heckspoiler grundiert"). [S2, MEDIUM] Next to it the catalogue lists a **painted** version, number 51710018269 ("Heckspoiler lackiert, CODE - UNI/MET."), painted to your colour code. [S2, MEDIUM] No option condition is printed for it: the catalogue doesn't tie the lip to the M package or to any other option. [S2, MEDIUM]

Its part page lists every European Coupé model, from the 316Ci to the 330Cd, and the M3. It doesn't list the M3 CSL, which BMW's catalogue treats as its own model. [S2, MEDIUM] The Sedan has its own boot spoiler in BMW's catalogue, a different part; don't buy one for the other. [S2, MEDIUM]

## What BMW called it

BMW's German price lists name the Coupé's rear spoiler as part of the Clubsport package, and the name moves around:
- **September 2002:** an "M Heckspoiler" (M rear spoiler). [S1, MEDIUM]
- **March 2004:** a "freistehender Heckflügel", a free-standing rear wing. [S1, MEDIUM]
- **September 2004:** a "Heckspoilerlippe", a rear spoiler lip, as standard, with a separate rear wing as an option (number 754, €650, only with the Clubsport). [S1, HIGH]

So "the Clubsport spoiler" can mean a lip or a wing, depending on the year. Whether the one spoiler in BMW's catalogue is the lip or the wing isn't settled by our sources. [S2, MEDIUM] E46BUILD treats it as the lip, the same small lip our Coupé M-rear picture shows.

## Primed or painted

- **Primed** is the version you'll find for sale new. When we checked European BMW parts dealers on 9 November 2026, two had it in stock, at about €193 and €209. [S3, MEDIUM] It needs painting in your car's colour before it goes on.
- **Painted** sounds easier, but only one dealer listed it, at about €158: cheaper than primed. There was no way to enter a colour online, so the colour code has to be arranged with the dealer, and that dealer doesn't take back painted parts. [S3, MEDIUM]

If you go painted, give the dealer your paint code in writing and get the colour confirmed before you pay. The code is on your car's paint plate. For most owners, primed plus a local paint shop is the safer route.

## Buying one: what to check

- **That it's the Coupé part.** A genuine BMW lip names 51717893671 (primed) or 51710018269 (painted) on the listing; the Sedan's boot spoiler is a different part. [S2, MEDIUM]
- **That it's BMW's own.** Aftermarket lips sold as an "M3 look" or "CSL look" aren't BMW parts, even when they look alike.
- **That your Coupé is on the list.** BMW's catalogue lists every European Coupé model and the M3, but not the CSL. [S2, MEDIUM]
- **Used:** a used lip comes in its donor car's colour, and at the time of our check a used one cost more than a new primed one. [S3, MEDIUM]
