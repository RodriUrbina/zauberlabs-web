A used E46 can look right and still be the wrong car for the parts you want. Most of the surprises come from a few questions nobody asked before money changed hands. Here are ten, each with the reason and a link to the article that explains it. [S1]

## The car

**1. When was it built, to the month?** Not the registration year. BMW changed the E46 on specific build months, and the year most owners know is the year of first registration, which can be months later. Our [build-month article](/en/blog/e46-build-month) lists the changeovers. [S1]

**2. Was it built for Europe or the US?** BMW's catalogue, as read on our mirror, keeps separate trees for the two, and some parts carry different numbers (MEDIUM in that article): see [The US-market E46](/en/blog/e46-us-market-catalogue), and for headlights [Same car, different continent](/en/blog/e46-us-vs-european-headlights). [S1]

**3. What's the paint code?** Not just the colour name. BMW's names differ by language and some carry numerals, and BMW's catalogue, as read on our mirror, sells painted parts by code (MEDIUM in that article). See [Japan Red to Laguna Seca Blue](/en/blog/e46-paint-code). [S1]

**4. Which packages did it leave the factory with?** Ask for the factory option list. The codes to look for: 338 for the M Sport package II, 768 for the Clubsport, 722, 723 or 724 for the Editions. [S2, HIGH] What each one contained is in our articles on [the M Sport package II](/en/blog/e46-m-sport-package-ii), [the Clubsport Coupé](/en/blog/e46-clubsport-coupe) and [the Editions](/en/blog/e46-edition-models). Parts can be swapped, so the parts on the car aren't proof. [S1]

## The steering wheel and the lights

**5. Is the airbag steering wheel the one listed for this car?** The wheel is the driver airbag, and on the Sedan, Touring and Coupé, BMW's catalogue as read on our mirror changes the generation around June 2000 (MEDIUM in that article). Ask for the part numbers. See [June 2000](/en/blog/e46-june-2000-airbag-wheels) and [Your E46 steering wheel is an airbag](/en/blog/e46-steering-wheel-swaps). [S1]

**6. Any warning lights, or stored fault codes?** A generic OBD scanner reads the emission faults; airbag and ABS faults usually need a tool that speaks BMW's diagnosis. See [OBD scanners and the E46](/en/blog/e46-obd-scanners). [S1]

**7. Which headlights does it have?** Halogen, xenon, bi-xenon or adaptive, and which side of the facelift. See [How to tell which E46 headlight you have](/en/blog/e46-headlight-technology-spot-yours) and [Two dates decide every E46 headlight](/en/blog/e46-headlight-changeovers). [S1]

## Wheels, seats and the rest

**8. Are the wheels genuine, and what size are they?** The size cast into the back of the rim settles a factory wheel. See [Genuine or replica?](/en/blog/e46-wheel-listing-genuine-or-replica) and [Will that wheel fit your E46?](/en/blog/will-that-wheel-fit-your-e46). In Germany, non-BMW wheels also need papers that name the car: [Aftermarket wheels in Germany](/en/blog/e46-aftermarket-wheels-germany). [S1]

**9. What are the seats, by name?** "Leather" or "Alcantara" isn't enough; BMW sold upholsteries by name. See [Cloth, leather or Alcantara?](/en/blog/e46-cloth-leather-alcantara) and, for a seat swap, [The airbag is in the door](/en/blog/e46-seat-swap-airbags). [S1]

**10. Before or after the facelift?** It decides headlights, bumpers and more, and the date depends on the body. See [The 2001 facelift](/en/blog/e46-2001-facelift). [S1]

## Buying parts, not a car

The same questions apply when you buy parts for your E46: the build month, the market, the paint code and the part number decide whether a part is the one BMW listed for your car. How to read a parts listing is in [Before you pay](/en/blog/buying-genuine-e46-parts-in-europe). [S1]
