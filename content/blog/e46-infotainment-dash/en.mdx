Every E46 dash holds one of two things: a BMW radio, or BMW's screen. Which one yours has decides almost everything else in this article. So before the catalogue, look at the dash.

## Radio or screen: look first

A **radio car** has a BMW radio in the slot. One you'll often see is the Business CD radio: a CD slot, one display and six station keys. [S2, HIGH]

A **screen car** has BMW's on-board monitor in the dash, and its radio isn't there at all: it sits in the boot. The cassette, MiniDisc or CD drive lives in the monitor's housing instead. [S2, HIGH] [S3, MEDIUM]

The screen changed shape in 2001. BMW's press kit for model year 2002 announced a wide-screen on-board monitor "for the first time on all 3 Series models". [S1, HIGH] BMW's training gives the numbers: 6.5 inches, 16:9, replacing the 5-inch 4:3 monitor. [S2, HIGH]

**The date:** 09/2001 for most E46s. The Convertible is the awkward one: BMW's training says the Cabrio got the wide screen from 03/2001, while the parts catalogue lists it from 09/2001. [S2, HIGH] [S3, MEDIUM] For a Convertible built between March and August 2001, look at the screen, not the build date.

One detail saves a wasted afternoon. BMW's "New Generation" radios, from 03/2001, have no anti-theft code, and they work only on the car's bus with at least one other module. [S2, HIGH] A used one needs no code, and it won't play on a workbench.

## Navigation Professional: one screen, two generations

On a wide-screen car, BMW's navigation looks the same whichever generation you have. The difference sits in the boot, on the left: BMW's training puts the navigation computer there. [S5, HIGH]

BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, lists two computers for the wide screen: the **Mk III** with maps on CD up to 09/2002, and the **Mk IV** with a DVD drive from 09/2002. [S3, MEDIUM] By the catalogue, then, a car built from 09/2001 to 08/2002 has the Mk III, and one built from 09/2002 the Mk IV.

**Adding navigation to a screen car** is a real job, but the screen stays. BMW's training lists what the navigation computer needs:
- a GPS antenna on its own cable: under the rear parcel shelf on the Sedan and Coupé, above the rear window under the spoiler on the Touring, behind the instrument cluster on the Convertible;
- a speed signal and a reverse signal;
- coding and software. With the Mk III computer, wide-screen cars need navigation software CD V16.1 or later. [S5, HIGH]

**Adding the wide screen to a radio car** is where owners get surprised. The only BMW navigation retrofit kit we found puts a colour monitor in the centre console where the ashtray was, for left-hand-drive Sedans only, and keeps the factory radio. [S4, HIGH] We found no BMW kit that puts the wide screen into a radio car's dash. Doing it means a new dash unit, a radio in the boot, the navigation computer, a GPS antenna and the wiring between them. [S2, HIGH] [S5, HIGH] It's a job for a specialist, not an evening.

## Harman Kardon: not a plug-in

BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, lists three speaker systems for the E46: Stereo, HiFi and Harman Kardon. Each has its own wiring harness. [S3, MEDIUM] Harman Kardon adds its own amplifier, plus a subwoofer amplifier and subwoofers in the parcel shelf on the Sedan, Coupé, Convertible and M3. [S3, MEDIUM] So it isn't a box you add; it's speakers, harness, amplifier and subwoofer.

We found no BMW retrofit kit for it. And in the catalogue tree we read for the Compact, there is no Harman Kardon group at all, only a HiFi amplifier. [S3, MEDIUM]

## BMW Bluetooth: calls, not music

BMW made a Bluetooth hands-free kit for every E46 body. [S4, HIGH] Its terms are strict:
- cars **from 03/2002** only;
- **telephone preparation (factory option 640)** required;
- coded by a BMW workshop afterwards, in about two hours of fitting;
- the phone adapter is sold separately. [S4, HIGH]

BMW calls it a hands-free kit, and that is what it is: the instructions describe calls. Playing music over Bluetooth isn't mentioned anywhere in them. [S4, HIGH]

The kits for the Sedan, Coupé and Compact list the Business and Business CD radios, the Reverse radio and BMW's screens. They don't name the Professional radio. [S4, HIGH]

## AUX: a jack, from 09/2002

BMW's AUX input is the simplest upgrade in this article: about an hour and a half, and no coding. [S4, HIGH] It plays through the radio, so volume and tone stay on the BMW controls, and traffic announcements interrupt it. [S4, HIGH]

It is valid only for cars **from 09/2002** with the **Business CD radio** or **BMW's screen, with or without navigation**. Either way the jack goes in the glove box; on a screen car its cable plugs in at the monitor, on a Business CD car at the radio. [S4, HIGH] BMW doesn't list its other radios for it: the cassette radios, the Reverse, the Professional and the radio-navigation system. [S4, HIGH]

## Modern touchscreens: what you keep, what you give up

Several makers sell touchscreens made for the E46, in 7-inch and 9-inch versions, that fill the factory opening and add Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. Everything in this section is what the makers say, not our verdict. [S6, LOW]

- **Fit:** the makers say their units fit every E46 body and year, including the M3. [S6, LOW]
- **Screen cars:** at least one maker sells an extra harness for cars that had BMW's screen, because the radio sits in the boot. [S6, LOW]
- **What they say you keep:** the steering-wheel buttons, the parking sensors and a Harman Kardon amplifier. [S6, LOW] On an E46 the steering-wheel buttons talk to the radio over the car's bus, so a new unit keeps them only through its own bus interface. [S2, HIGH]
- **What you give up:** BMW's navigation and TV, if fitted, and the factory look. On a screen car you also lose the screen's on-board computer menu; the one in the instrument cluster stays. [S2] [S3, MEDIUM]

If you'd rather keep the BMW radio, adapters connect behind the BMW radio and add Bluetooth and USB, according to their product pages. [S6, LOW] Your dash doesn't change at all.

**Before you buy:** confirm with the seller that the unit and its harness suit your car, and whether it ships to your country.
