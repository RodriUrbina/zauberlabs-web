A painted part is only as good as the paint code you give the seller. The E46 makes that harder than it sounds: BMW gave the same colours different names in English and German, added numerals to some, and none of the BMW documents we hold prints the codes. Here is how BMW named the E46's paints, why the code matters, and where owners say you'll find it.

## Why the code matters

BMW sells body parts in two ways: primed, to be painted by you, or painted to your car's colour. BMW's parts catalogue, as read on our mirror, lists the Coupé's rear spoiler painted as "lackiert, CODE - UNI/MET.", painted to your code, whether uni or metallic. [S3, MEDIUM] Trim parts that come in the car's colour carry the note "When ordering indicate color code". [S3, MEDIUM] The colour name alone isn't enough, for the reasons below. How the spoiler is bought primed or painted is in [The Coupé's rear M spoiler lip](/en/blog/e46-coupe-rear-m-spoiler-lip).

## Where owners find it

No BMW document in our research says where the paint code is on an E46. What owners say on E46 forums, as their posts show up in web search, is consistent: look for a sticker on a strut tower under the bonnet. Most posts point to the driver's side, some to the other side, so check both. The sticker is said to show the colour's name and number, and to be easy to miss under dirt. [S4, LOW] The forum pages themselves block automated reading, so we can't link a post we've read in full.

If there's no sticker, the usual advice is that a BMW dealer can look up the factory colour from your car's vehicle identification number (VIN). That's general advice, not a BMW statement we can cite. [S4, LOW]

## One colour, two names

BMW's English and German documents name the same colours differently. What BMW wrote, by market:

| English, as BMW wrote it | German, as BMW wrote it |
| --- | --- |
| Japan Red (UK, 2001) | japanrot II (price lists from 2004) |
| Titanium Silver (UK, 2002) | titansilber (price lists) |
| Estoril Blue, Velvet Blue (UK, 2002) | estorilblau, velvetblau (price lists) |
| Laguna Seca Blue (USA, 2000) | Laguna-seca Blau (2006) |
| Phoenix Yellow Metallic (USA, 2000) | Phönixgelb (2006) |

From BMW's press releases [S2, HIGH] and German price lists. [S1, HIGH]

- **Japan Red** was new for the Sedan and Touring with the 2001 facelift, together with Tourmaline Violet and Satin Beige, according to BMW's UK press kit. [S2, HIGH]
- **Titanium Silver, Estoril Blue and Velvet Blue** are the three colours BMW's UK release names for the 2002 Clubsport Coupé. [S2, HIGH]
- **Laguna Seca Blue and Phoenix Yellow Metallic** are, in [BMW of North America's November 2000 release](https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/americas/the-new-m3-convertible---bmw-m-performance-plus-open-air-pleasure), "Two new colors … shared with the M3 models". [S2, HIGH] [BMW's German press](https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/global/de/der-bmw-m3---eine-sportlegende-wird-20-jahre-alt--bmw-praesentiert-zum-jubilaeum-ein-exklusives-miniaturen-set) wrote them as Phönixgelb and Laguna-seca Blau in 2006, for a set of miniatures of the E46 M3 Coupé and Convertible. [S2, HIGH]
- **Interlagos Blue** is, in a 2022 BMW release, "taken from the palette of the storied E46 M3". [S2, HIGH]

**The numerals.** BMW's German price lists write some names with a numeral: schwarz II, alpinweiß III, imolarot II, japanrot II, oxfordgrün II. [S1, HIGH] The lists don't say what the numeral means. That's one more reason to order by code, not by name.

## Uni, metallic, Individual, M

BMW's German price lists sort the E46's paints into four kinds. [S1, HIGH]
- **Uni** (solid) paints. In the September 2004 Coupé list, for example: imolarot, schwarz and alpinweiß.
- **Metallic**, at extra cost: €650 in the 2002 lists, €670 from March 2004, €750 from September 2005 on the Coupé, Convertible and M3.
- **BMW Individual** paints, €1,700 from March 2004, with "BMW Individual" door sills included.
- **BMW M** paints, also €1,700.

In the September 2004 Coupé list, both Individual and M paints come only with white front indicators. [S1, HIGH]

Some colours were tied to packages. BMW's 2003 press kit says chiarettorot was reserved for the Edition models, and the Clubsport had its own short list of metallics. [S2, HIGH] [S1, HIGH] So the paint can also be a clue to how a car was ordered.

## Before you order a painted part

1. **Find the code** on the sticker, or have it looked up from your VIN.
2. **Give the code in writing,** not only the name. Names vary by language, and some carry numerals.
3. **Get the colour confirmed** before you pay. A painted part may not be returnable.
4. **If in doubt, buy primed** and have a local paint shop match your car.

## Notes on the evidence

**Confidence.** The paint names and surcharges come from BMW's own documents: its German price lists (HIGH, Germany only) and its press releases (HIGH, each for its own market). That painted parts are sold by colour code comes from one mirror of BMW's parts catalogue (MEDIUM). Where the sticker sits comes only from owner forums, read through search results (LOW).

**Not established.** Any E46 paint code number: none of the BMW documents we hold prints one. Where BMW itself puts the label. A complete list of E46 colours by year; the price lists refer to a colour card we don't have. What the numerals in the German names mean.
