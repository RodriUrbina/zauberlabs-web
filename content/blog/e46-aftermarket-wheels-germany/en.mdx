On a German E46, a wheel that fits isn't automatically a wheel you may drive on. It also needs papers, and the papers have to name your car. This is how that works, as the regulation and the approvals themselves set it out. It's an explainer, not legal advice: for your car, the inspector at the HU and the papers themselves decide.

## Why a wheel needs papers

Every car on German roads has an operating permit, the *Betriebserlaubnis*. The road-vehicle licensing regulation, the [StVZO, § 19](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__19.html), is generally read like this: [S2, LOW]
- the permit **lapses** if a change is made that can be expected to endanger other road users;
- it does **not** lapse when the new part has its own approval, and any restrictions or fitting instructions in that approval are followed;
- if those restrictions aren't followed, it lapses after all.

So the question for an aftermarket wheel is never just "does it bolt on?". It's "is there an approval that covers this wheel on this car, and am I following it?"

## The papers a wheel can come with

- **ABE.** A German approval for the wheel as a part, under [§ 22 StVZO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__22.html). It can be limited to certain vehicles, and it can make its validity depend on an inspection of the fitted wheel, which the inspector then confirms on the document. [S2, LOW]
- **Teilegutachten.** The old route, a technical report. According to the German approval authority, the KBA, it is replaced by the national part type approval (*Teiletypgenehmigung*): no new ones after 19 June 2025, and parts with one may still be sold and used until 19 June 2028. [S2, LOW]
- **An EU or UN-ECE approval** for the wheel, which the regulation also recognises when its restrictions are followed. [S2, LOW]
- **Nothing that covers your car.** Then only an individual approval of the whole car by an officially recognised inspector is left, under [§ 21 StVZO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__21.html), with an outcome nobody can promise you. [S2, LOW]

## What an approval for an E46 looks like

Our research read the approvals of four aftermarket wheels, in 16 and 17 inches. [S1, HIGH] They show what to look for.

- **The car is named by type.** The vehicle list names the E46 by its type codes, 346L, 346C, 346R, 346K and 346X, each with a power range in kW. [S1, HIGH]
- **Tyre sizes are listed per car and axle.** A size that isn't listed isn't approved. [S1, HIGH]
- **Conditions come as codes.** Typical ones in these documents: an inspection by an officially recognised expert, arch work such as rolling the arch edges or trimming the liners, or updating your registration papers with the new tyre size. [S1, HIGH]
- **The bolts are specified,** with their thread, shank length, seat and torque for each car. [S1, HIGH]
- **Standard brakes are assumed.** Upgraded brakes take the car outside the approval. [S1, HIGH]

The four documents show the whole range. [S1, HIGH]
- One 16-inch wheel bolts onto an E46 but doesn't list a single E46 type, so it has no approval for one.
- One 17-inch wheel lists every E46 type, but every E46 tyre row needs an expert's inspection, and most need arch work.
- Another 17-inch wheel lists the E46 with no inspection and no bodywork on its base sizes: buy, fit, carry the papers.
- A third lists the base sizes without conditions, but its wider tyres need an inspection and arch work.

In one of these approvals, the power range runs from 77 to 170 kW: the 316i to the 330i and 330Ci, but not the M3. [S1, HIGH] Check the M3's power against the range, whatever the type code says.

On a used wheel, the approval number, type, size and offset are stamped on the wheel; that's how a wheel from a private sale can be matched to its document. [S1, HIGH]

## Carry the papers

The regulation says the driver has to carry a copy of the approval, or of the extract that matters for the car, and show it on request, unless the vehicle registration certificate already records the change. [S2, LOW] If the approval demanded an inspection, you carry the confirmation of that inspection instead. [S2, LOW]

## At the HU

The periodic inspection, the HU, is required by [§ 29 StVZO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__29.html) and its annexes. [S2, LOW] In the HU guideline's defect list, as our research read it: [S2, LOW]
- **A lapsed operating permit** is a dangerous defect.
- **A positive inspection report that isn't yet in your papers** is a significant defect.

Either way, no new HU sticker until it's sorted.

## A short checklist

1. **Find the approval** for the exact wheel: approval number, type, size and offset, as stamped on the wheel.
2. **Check your car is on it:** the type code and the power in kW.
3. **Check your tyre size** is listed for your car and axle.
4. **Read the conditions:** an inspection, arch work, updated papers.
5. **Keep the papers in the car,** or have the change recorded in your registration certificate.

For whether a wheel physically fits before any of this, see [Will that wheel fit your E46?](/en/blog/will-that-wheel-fit-your-e46). For BMW's own wheels, the factory sizes are in [Six wheels, three families](/en/blog/telling-e46-wheels-apart).
